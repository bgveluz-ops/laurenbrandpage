About

The practitioner’s case for building on purpose.

Lauren Rosa Miller is an entrepreneur, strategist, speaker, and legacy architect whose work spans entrepreneurship, public-sector strategy, economic development, culture, and generational stewardship.

A Howard University graduate and fourth-generation entrepreneur, Lauren is a fifth-generation descendant of a family whose history of landownership and agriculture in Southwest Georgia spans nearly 150 years. Her understanding of ownership, opportunity, and responsibility was shaped long before she began building platforms around those ideas.

In 2013, Lauren founded Can’t Stay Put, a travel and lifestyle platform that encouraged a generation to explore the world, embrace possibility, and create lives rooted in purpose. What began as a personal vision grew into a community of more than 25,000 people, earning national media attention and establishing her as an early voice in the emerging Black millennial travel movement.

Her professional journey also includes helping lead the next chapter of Miller³ Consulting, the public-sector procurement and data analytics firm founded by her father in 1986. Today, as Principal and Chief Growth Officer, Lauren works at the intersection of institutional strategy, economic opportunity, and business growth.

But some of her most consequential lessons came through navigating her father’s illness, caregiving, and the complexities of family business succession. Those experiences deepened her understanding of what it takes to preserve what previous generations have built while preparing the next generation to carry it forward.

They also inspired her to establish In the Room With Legacy, a platform dedicated to changing how we think about family and legacy and how we organize ourselves around what we’ve been called to build, steward, and grow across generations.

Across her work, Lauren brings a perspective shaped by entrepreneurship, institutional leadership, family history, and lived experience. Her message is consistent: what we build matters, but so does how we prepare it to last.

“I didn’t learn succession planning in a classroom. I learned it while my father was ill, the business still had to run, and no one had written any of it down.”

Signature Talks

Conversations that challenge how we think about identity, opportunity, leadership, wealth, and what we leave behind. Drawing from entrepreneurship, institutional strategy, family history, and lived experience, Lauren delivers keynotes and conversations that connect big ideas to the choices we make in our lives, organizations, and communities.

01 Family Is the First InstitutionWhy the future of wealth, leadership, and legacy begins at home.

Before there were corporations, universities, or governments, there was family. Yet we often devote more attention to building professional institutions than strengthening the one responsible for shaping generations.

Drawing from nearly 150 years of family land stewardship and her experience navigating the succession of a multigenerational business, Lauren challenges audiences to rethink the family as an institution worthy of vision, governance, investment, and intentional leadership.

Audience takeaway: A new understanding of how families can operate with greater intention to preserve and transfer more than financial wealth.

02 The Wealth You Cannot SeeRethinking what we inherit, what we value, and what we pass forward.

When we talk about generational wealth, the conversation often begins and ends with money. But some of the most consequential inheritances never appear on a balance sheet.

Through personal storytelling and a multidimensional framework for understanding wealth, Lauren explores how families preserve and transfer relationships, knowledge, values, cultural identity, opportunity, and other forms of capital. She challenges audiences to recognize the wealth they may already possess and become more intentional about what they are creating for future generations.

Audience takeaway: A broader understanding of wealth and practical ways to identify, strengthen, and transfer nonfinancial assets.

03 Succession Is Not an EventPreparing the next generation to inherit responsibility, not just assets.

Too often, succession planning begins when a transition is already underway. But preserving a business, an estate, or a family institution requires more than transferring legal ownership.

Drawing from her experience as a caregiver, successor, and co-owner of a multigenerational business, Lauren explores the human and operational dimensions of succession, from institutional knowledge and leadership preparation to family expectations and shared vision. She challenges audiences to think beyond preserving assets and consider how to preserve the purpose that made those assets meaningful.

Audience takeaway: A more comprehensive understanding of succession and the importance of preparing people, not simply transferring property.

04 Build a Life You Don’t Have to Escape FromWhat entrepreneurship, exploration, and intentional living can teach us about designing a life of possibility.

Long before entrepreneurship and content creation became mainstream career paths, Lauren founded Can’t Stay Put, a travel and lifestyle platform built around a simple idea: we have more agency over the lives we create than we often realize.

Drawing from her experience building a platform, exploring the world, and navigating the evolution of her own identity and ambitions, Lauren challenges audiences to examine the expectations they’ve inherited, the possibilities they’ve overlooked, and the courage required to build a life that reflects who they are becoming.

Audience takeaway: A renewed sense of agency and practical inspiration to make more intentional choices about how they live, lead, and pursue opportunity.

05 Know Where You Come FromHow identity, heritage, and storytelling shape belonging, leadership, and the futures we imagine.

Knowing where we come from can fundamentally change how we understand who we are and what we’re capable of becoming.

Drawing from her family’s nearly 150-year history of land stewardship in southwest Georgia, Lauren explores the role of family history in shaping identity, confidence, belonging, and generational possibility. This talk challenges audiences to see family stories not simply as memories of the past, but as resources that can inform decisions, strengthen relationships, and shape the future.

Audience takeaway: A renewed appreciation for family history as a source of identity, connection, and intergenerational wisdom.

06 From Opportunity to OwnershipBuilding businesses and economic ecosystems positioned for sustainable growth.

Creating more businesses is not the same as creating more economic opportunity. Too often, entrepreneurship is encouraged without sufficient attention to market demand, institutional spending, business readiness, and the pathways that allow enterprises to grow and endure.

Drawing from her experience in public-sector procurement, economic development, and multigenerational business leadership, Lauren challenges audiences to think beyond business creation toward building and acquiring enterprises positioned to compete, scale, create jobs, and generate lasting economic value.

Audience takeaway: A more strategic understanding of how entrepreneurship, institutional opportunity, and business ownership can work together to create sustainable economic growth.

Specialized Speaking Offering
Don’t Wait Until Someone Gets SickThe conversations families need to have before a crisis.

At 25, Lauren became a caregiver for her father while navigating the responsibilities of family, business, and an uncertain future. That experience fundamentally changed how she understood love, responsibility, preparation, and legacy.

In this deeply personal talk, Lauren explores the conversations families often postpone until illness, loss, or an unexpected transition forces them to confront difficult decisions. Through storytelling and practical insights, she encourages audiences to begin conversations about family history, wishes, responsibilities, and succession while they still have the opportunity to do so together.

Audience takeaway: Greater confidence and urgency to initiate meaningful family conversations before a crisis makes them unavoidable.
